Ideas and buying advice

Gift ideas for space lovers

Would they rather read about a mission, build a rocket model or spend a clear evening finding constellations? Start there. A telescope is one option, but a sky map or a book about the subject they keep coming back to may be the gift they use more.

Before you choose

Narrow down
the options.

  • Find out whether they want to look at the sky or learn about it indoors. Outdoor equipment also needs storage and somewhere suitable to use it.
  • For a telescope, ease of setup and a steady mount matter more than an impressive magnification figure. Ask an enthusiast what they need before adding equipment.
  • Check the age guidance on model kits and allow for adult help, tools and a place to leave a half-finished build.

01

Glow-in-the-dark star stickers

A space-themed bedroom detail for someone who would like stars above their bed.

What to look forAsk permission before buying anything adhesive for a child's room. Compare sticker sizes and surface guidance; choose a constellation layout if they would enjoy arranging recognisable patterns.

02

Beginner telescope

For someone who wants to spend time learning their way around the night sky.

What to look forLook for a stable mount and clear setup instructions, and consider where it will be stored. Do not buy on magnification alone. Never point a telescope at the sun.

03

Solar system model kit

Build the planets in order, then find a place for the finished solar system.

What to look forA hanging model needs different space from a tabletop one. Use the finished dimensions to choose, then allow for any paint, glue or tools listed separately and follow the kit's age guidance.

04

Illustrated book about space

Choose a subject they keep asking about: planets, space missions or what lies beyond our solar system.

What to look forRead a sample to judge the level of detail, and check the publication date for recent missions. A child learning planet names and an adult interested in astronomy need different books.

05

Planisphere

Turn the discs to the date and time to see which constellations to look for.

What to look forChoose a chart for UK latitudes, with labels large enough to read in dim light. A rotating planisphere is for using outdoors; a decorative constellation print serves a different purpose.

06

Rocket display model kit

A building project for someone interested in spacecraft, with a model to keep at the end.

What to look forChoose a non-launching display model and match the skill level. Check the age guidance, finished height and whether glue, paint or tools must be bought separately.

07

Space-themed jigsaw

Planets, spacecraft or a crowded star field to piece together on a cloudy evening.

What to look forThe image affects difficulty as much as the piece count: a large area of dark sky can be surprisingly demanding. Allow enough table space for the finished puzzle and some room for sorting.

08

Binoculars for stargazing

An option for exploring the sky without setting up a telescope each time.

What to look forConsider weight as well as magnification: they need to be comfortable to hold, or used with a suitable support. Check the return policy for fit. Never point binoculars at the sun.
