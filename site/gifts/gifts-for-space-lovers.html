Gift ideas for space lovers

Space fans range from eight-year-olds who know every planet to adults who stargaze from the garden. Match the gift to how hands-on they like to be.

Before you choose

A few things
worth knowing.

  • For children, a simple kit they can build themselves is often more fun than something fragile.
  • For adults, a good star chart or binoculars can beat a cheap telescope.
  • Check recommended ages on kits that include small parts or tools.

Illustrated book about space

A good choice for the endlessly curious person with one more question.

What to look forMatch the reading level and check the publication date. Adult observers may prefer a practical sky guide to a general children's encyclopaedia.

05

Planisphere or star map

A screen-free way to work out what is overhead on a clear night.

What to look forChoose one suitable for UK latitudes. A readable rotating planisphere is more practical outdoors than a purely decorative constellation print.

Rocket building kit

An afternoon project for the person fascinated by how things fit together.

What to look forDistinguish a display model from a launching kit. Check tools, consumables, recommended ages and any outdoor-space or supervision requirements.
