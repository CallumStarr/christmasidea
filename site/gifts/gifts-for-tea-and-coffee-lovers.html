Gift ideas for tea and coffee lovers

The most useful clue is how they make their usual drink. Loose-leaf tea, filter coffee and espresso need different equipment, and an enthusiast may already have a setup they like. Check what they use before adding to it.

The morning ritual

the options.

  • Check the brewing method before buying filters, beans or accessories. For ground coffee, the grind needs to suit their equipment.
  • For mugs and travel cups, look at the size they actually use and whether they want a handle, a bag-safe lid or dishwasher cleaning.
  • Ask about caffeine, flavours and dietary requirements before choosing a tasting set. Decaf and unflavoured options are worth checking too.

Pour-over coffee dripper set

For someone who enjoys weighing, pouring and adjusting their morning brew.

What to look forStart with the brewer they use now, so you do not duplicate it. Filter shape and size vary between drippers; choose one with readily available filters that fits their mug or server.

Insulated travel mug

For a slow coffee drinker or a journey that takes them away from the kettle.

What to look forChoose for the journey: a cup-holder mug can have a different lid from one intended for a bag. Look for a closure suited to how they carry it and a lid that comes apart for cleaning.

Loose-leaf teapot with infuser

Make enough loose-leaf tea for a second cup or someone else at the table.

What to look forWork in cups rather than the size of the pot in a photograph. A removable basket lets them take the leaves out once brewed, and a wide opening makes washing up easier.

04

Hand coffee grinder

For a coffee drinker who wants to grind a small batch of beans just before brewing.

What to look forMatch the adjustment range to their brewing method, especially for espresso. Check how much it holds and how it is adjusted; manual grinding also takes time and effort they may not want.

Handmade ceramic mug

Choose a shape and glaze they would enjoy using for their usual tea or coffee.

What to look forCompare capacity and handle shape with a mug they already like. Confirm dishwasher and microwave suitability, and check whether you are buying the pictured item or one with handmade variations.

06

Handheld milk frother

A small whisk for adding frothed milk to a home coffee or hot chocolate.

What to look forThis type whisks milk but does not heat it. Choose a jug-style machine instead if they want both jobs done automatically; for a handheld whisk, allow for batteries if they are sold separately.

07

Tea tasting set

Small portions let a tea drinker try several blends without committing to a large tin.

What to look forMatch bagged or loose-leaf tea to what they use, then check caffeine, flavourings and allergens. Read how many servings are included; gift-box size tells you little about the amount of tea.

Coffee storage canister

A dedicated home for the beans or grounds they buy regularly.

What to look forMatch the capacity to their usual bag of coffee. An easy-to-open seal is useful every morning; an oversized canister takes up cupboard space without helping someone who buys small batches.
