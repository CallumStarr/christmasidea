Gift ideas for book lovers

Think about where you usually find them with a book. A paperback tucked into a commuting bag, a hardback at the kitchen table and an e-reader at bedtime call for different extras. These ideas work best when they fit the way someone already reads.

  • Check whether they mostly read paper books, e-books or audiobooks before choosing an accessory.
  • For anything that holds or covers a book, measure the format they actually use. A large hardback can rule out a sleeve or support.
  • If you would rather give a book, use their wish list or ask for a recommendation in a subject they enjoy. Keep a gift receipt to avoid duplicates.

01

Clip-on reading light

Light that can travel with a paper book from the armchair to the bedside.

What to look forA light clip is kinder to paperback pages than a heavy lamp pulling on the cover. Look for an adjustable low setting for bedtime and a power source they can recharge or replace easily.

02

Flat bookmark

A favourite colour, book cover or illustration to mark their place.

What to look forKeep it thin enough for the book to close comfortably. Thick charms and long decorations can get in the way inside a bag, even when they look attractive in the gift box.

03

Padded book sleeve

A cover for the paperback that shares a bag with keys, lunch and everything else.

What to look forMeasure a book they carry and compare the sleeve's internal dimensions, leaving room for its thickness. 'Standard book size' varies between sellers; a closure helps keep the book inside.

04

Book-support cushion

A resting place for a heavy book during a long reading session.

What to look forAsk whether they would use a support before choosing one. The angle and page-holding design need to suit their reading position and book size, and a removable washable cover is useful.

05

Literary tote bag

Something to take to the library, with a design from a book or writer they love.

What to look forThink about the books going inside: a tall hardback needs depth, and a full bag needs sturdy stitching. Choose handles long enough for how they carry it; an inner pocket can keep keys separate.

06

Bookends

For a shelf of books that needs support at one end or room to grow.

What to look forMatch the height and weight to their books and check shelf depth. A non-marking base is useful, but very light decorative bookends may not support a row of large hardbacks.

07

Reading throw

A blanket for reading at home when the story is good but the room is chilly.

What to look forChoose enough length to cover their legs rather than a small decorative square. A familiar fabric and a straightforward care label make this easier to use than a delicate throw that needs special washing.

08

Reading journal

Somewhere to remember a favourite line, record a recommendation or work out what to read next.

What to look forLook inside: some journals are full of ratings and targets, while others leave room for notes. Choose the layout that suits them, with enough entries for the amount they read.
