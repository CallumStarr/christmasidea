Ad · Gift ideas for grandparents

By Callum Starr, NOAH Neurotech Ltd · Updated 7 November 2026

A gift for a grandparent works best when it fits the way they already spend their time: reading, keeping in touch, pottering in the garden or having the family round. Ask a parent or another relative what they use every day. These are gift types to look for within £30, not checked offers; include delivery in the total.

Ideas are selected from fixed gift-concept lists for the topic and budget, with practical buying checks. We have not bought or tested the products in these searches.

Time well spent

Narrow down
the options.

  • Think about eyesight, grip and hearing before choosing anything with small print, fiddly fastenings or quiet controls. A simpler version is often the one that gets used.
  • Find out what they already have. Grandparents are often given the same mug, frame or slippers by several people.
  • Black Friday listings can change quickly. Judge the delivered total against your £30 limit rather than the size of a claimed reduction, and check the returns window in case the gift needs exchanging after Christmas.

01

Wooden photo frame

A frame for a recent family photograph they would like on the mantelpiece.

What to look forCheck the print size the frame takes, not just the outside measurements, and whether it stands, hangs or both. Print the photograph before wrapping so it is ready to display.

02

Large-print puzzle book

Crosswords, word searches or number puzzles for a quiet half hour.

What to look forLook at sample pages: print size and grid size matter as much as the type of puzzle. Choose the puzzle type they already enjoy rather than a mixed book.

03

Clip-on book light

Extra light for reading in a favourite chair without changing the whole room.

What to look forCheck whether it uses batteries or a charging cable, and that the buttons are easy to find and press. The clip should grip a thick hardback without marking the pages.

04

Washable fleece throw

Something warm to keep over the knees in a favourite armchair.

What to look forChoose a size that covers the knees and feet, not just a decorative square. A throw that goes in the washing machine is easier to look after than one that needs special cleaning.

05

Window bird feeder

A way to watch garden birds from indoors on a cold morning.

What to look forCheck how it attaches and whether someone can reach it to refill and clean it. Suction cups need a smooth, clean pane; ask before fixing anything to their windows.

06

Large-piece jigsaw puzzle

A picture to work on over several afternoons, perhaps with the grandchildren.

What to look forMatch the piece count to puzzles they already finish, and check the finished size against their table. Larger pieces are easier to handle, and a clear reference picture helps.

07

Grandparent memory journal

A guided book for writing down stories and memories to pass on to the family.

What to look forRead sample pages: some books ask many short questions, while others leave space for longer stories. Choose one with room to write comfortably and paper that suits their usual pen.

08

Illuminated hand magnifier

Help with reading small print on labels, maps or puzzle books.

What to look forCheck the lens size and whether it needs batteries. Stronger magnification usually means a smaller viewing area, so ask whether they already use one and what they find awkward about it.
