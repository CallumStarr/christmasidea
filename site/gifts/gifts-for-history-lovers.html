Gift ideas for history lovers

Find out which period or place has caught their attention before searching for a history gift. A reader interested in Roman Britain may have little use for a general world-history book. The same clue can guide you towards a map, model or game if their shelves are already full.

  • Check their shelves or wish list. A newly published title can still repeat a subject they already know well.
  • Match how they enjoy history: reading about it, visiting places, building models or discussing it with other people.
  • For a map, chart or model, check the finished dimensions and display space. A frame or extra modelling materials may need a separate budget.

01

Narrative history book

Follow the people and decisions behind an event they want to understand better.

What to look forRead a sample from their chosen period. Some accounts tell a broad story; others follow one argument in depth. The introduction and bibliography help you judge which kind of reader the book is written for.

02

Historical atlas

A companion for following changing borders, journeys and the places mentioned in a history book.

What to look forStart with the region and period, then look at sample maps. Readable labels and an index make the book easier to use; fitting more maps into a pocket edition can come at the expense of detail.

03

Historical model kit

A project for someone who enjoys recreating a building, ship or scene from a particular era.

What to look forMatch the subject and skill level before choosing a scale. Check the finished dimensions and whether paints, glue and tools are included; the box price may not cover the whole build.

04

History trivia game

Put their accumulated facts to use over a game with friends or family.

What to look forRead some example questions: a broad world-history quiz and one centred on British monarchs will suit different groups. Choose a difficulty and playing time the other players will enjoy too.

05

Reproduction map print

Choose a place with a connection to them: a home town, a favourite city or a journey.

What to look forCheck the map date, area covered and print size. Make sure it is described as a reproduction, and find out whether a frame is included or needs to be bought separately.

06

History or museum jigsaw

Spend an afternoon piecing together a favourite painting, historic scene or collection.

What to look forA familiar image gives this more meaning than a generic historic scene. Match the piece count and finished size to puzzles they enjoy, and look for a separate reference picture to work from.

07

History timeline chart

See which events were happening at the same time in different parts of the world.

What to look forA chart with readable text is worth more than one that crams in every date. Choose the regions and period first, then a wall poster or folding format to suit how they will use it.

08

Biography of a historical figure

A closer look at someone from history they keep wanting to know more about.

What to look forAn accessible life story and an academic reassessment are different gifts. Read the introduction for the author's approach, and note the original publication date: a new cover may be a reissue.
