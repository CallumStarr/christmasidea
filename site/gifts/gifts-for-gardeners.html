Gift ideas for gardeners

Keen gardeners usually already own the basics, so the best gifts are quality upgrades, small luxuries or things for the quiet winter months.

Explore the eight ideas ↓How to choose
The outdoor edit

Before you choose

A few things
worth knowing.

  • Quality hand tools are a safe bet; a favourite trowel is used for years.
  • Avoid specific plants unless you know their garden and soil.
  • Seasonal extras such as seed collections and bird feeders suit almost every garden.

01

Stainless-steel hand trowel and fork

A worthwhile upgrade for the gardener still using the bent trowel from the back of the shed.

What to look forLook for a comfortable handle and a solid join between head and handle. A tool they enjoy holding beats a large set of flimsy extras.

02

Gardening gloves

A fresh pair for the person whose current ones have seen several winters.

What to look forCheck hand size and the kind of work they do. Fine planting and thorny pruning need different levels of protection; no glove is puncture-proof.

03

Seed collection

Next spring's project in a small envelope, especially for a cook who grows herbs.

What to look forCheck sowing dates, available growing space and whether the collection suits pots. Clearly labelled individual packets are easier to plan with.

04

Kneeler and seat

A practical option for someone who spends a long time planting or weeding at ground level.

What to look forCheck dimensions, weight limits and stability on their garden surface. Ask first rather than assuming an aid will suit their needs.

05

Hand cream for working hands

A small end-of-day ritual after washing off the soil.

What to look forCheck fragrance and ingredient preferences. A modest tube paired with a good nail brush can feel more considered than a generic gift basket.

06

Bird feeder

Something to watch from the kitchen window between gardening sessions.

What to look forChoose a feeder that opens easily for cleaning, with a suitable place to hang it. Check current wildlife guidance on hygiene and feeding.

07

Gardening journal

For the planting dates, unexpected successes and things to do differently next year.

What to look forLook for enough free-writing space and a practical size. An undated journal works better than one that starts halfway through the growing year.

08

Pruning snips

For picking herbs and cutting flowers without reaching for the kitchen scissors.

What to look forChoose fine snips for soft stems, not heavy branches. Check hand fit, blade protection and the closing catch; they are not a substitute for secateurs.
