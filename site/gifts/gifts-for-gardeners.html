Gift ideas for gardeners

Before buying a gardener another tool, find out what they reach for every weekend and what they wish worked better. Someone tending balcony pots has different needs from an allotment holder. These ideas include replacements for worn favourites and things to plan next season with.

  • Handle shape, weight and grip are personal. If you cannot try a tool with them, keep the receipt rather than assuming a larger set is an upgrade.
  • For plants or seeds, find out about light, soil and growing space. A packet suited to an allotment may be no use on a shaded windowsill.
  • Ask what needs replacing. A well-used pair of gloves or a full planting notebook gives you a better clue than a generic gardener's hamper.

01

Hand trowel and fork

A replacement for the worn hand tools they still reach for every weekend.

What to look forUse their favourite tools as a guide to handle shape and weight. A narrow trowel suits transplanting; a wider one suits digging. If possible, let them try the grip before you buy.

02

Gardening gloves

Useful to replace a tired pair, provided the size and type of work are right.

What to look forFine planting needs dexterity; handling thorny stems needs different protection. Check the size chart and intended use rather than relying on a one-size label or a general protection claim.

03

Seed collection

Next season's tomatoes, herbs or flowers, chosen for the space they have to grow them.

What to look forIndividual labelled packets let them plan when and where to sow. For pots, choose varieties described for containers; for an allotment, ask which crops they want to try next year.

04

Garden kneeler and seat

Worth asking about if they spend long stretches planting and weeding at ground level.

What to look forAsk whether they want a pad or a folding seat before buying. Check dimensions, weight limits and folding mechanism, and consider the paths and surfaces where they would use it.

05

Hand cream

An extra tube of a favourite cream to keep by the sink after gardening.

What to look forChoose a product they already use or ask about ingredients and fragrance. The 'gardener' label tells you less about whether it suits them than their existing tube does.

06

Bird feeder

A way to watch garden visitors from a favourite window, if feeding birds is something they want to take on.

What to look forCleaning is part of owning a feeder, so look for one that comes apart easily. Agree a suitable place to hang it, and consult current wildlife guidance on feeding and hygiene.

07

Gardening journal

Somewhere to record sowing dates, successful varieties and what to change next year.

What to look forLook inside before buying: planting plans, blank notes and harvest records serve different habits. An undated layout avoids wasted pages and lets them start whenever they choose.

08

Fine garden snips

A tool for picking herbs and cutting flowers without borrowing the kitchen scissors.

What to look forFine snips are for soft stems, not woody branches. A comfortable grip, an easy-to-use catch and a blade cover matter more here than a large tool set.
