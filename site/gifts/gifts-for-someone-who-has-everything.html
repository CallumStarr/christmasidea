Ad · Gift ideas for someone who has everything

By Callum Starr, NOAH Neurotech Ltd · Updated 7 November 2026

When someone already owns what they need, look for a new skill to try, a well-made version of something they use often, or something that brings the garden closer. Avoid adding clutter. These are gift types to look for within £30, not checked offers; include delivery in the total.

Ideas are selected from fixed gift-concept lists for the topic and budget, with practical buying checks. We have not bought or tested the products in these searches.

  • Look for the everyday item they use until it wears out, then choose a well-made version in a style they already like.
  • If you are unsure, choose something useful that can be put away after use rather than another ornament for a full shelf.
  • Black Friday listings can change quickly. Judge the delivered total against your £30 limit rather than the size of a claimed reduction, and check the returns window in case the gift needs exchanging after Christmas.

01

Beginner calligraphy set

A new skill to learn at the kitchen table over the winter.

What to look forCheck what is included: practice sheets and a guide matter as much as the pens. Brush pens are easier for a beginner than dip pens and bottled ink.

02

Kitchen scissors

A sturdy pair for herbs, packaging and string.

What to look forCheck whether they come apart for cleaning and whether they suit a left or right hand. A comfortable handle is more useful than a long list of extra functions.

03

Wooden bird nesting box

A home for garden birds that they can watch next spring.

What to look forCheck the entrance hole size against the birds they hope to attract, and where it can be fixed out of strong sun. Ask first if they rent or have no garden.

04

Wooden puzzle box

A small mechanical puzzle to solve, then use for keeping small treasures.

What to look forCheck the difficulty and whether a solution is included. Some boxes are mainly decorative; read the description to see how many moves it takes to open.

05

Illustrated non-fiction book

A book about a subject they have recently started asking about.

What to look forAsk what they have been reading or watching lately, and check their shelves for duplicates. Read sample pages to judge whether it suits a beginner or an expert.

06

Plant propagation station

A glass stand for growing new plants from cuttings on a windowsill.

What to look forCheck the size and number of glass tubes, and whether their windowsill is deep enough. Glass needs careful cleaning, and plants or cuttings are not included.

07

Engraved wooden chopping board

A board with a name or date, for someone who cooks often.

What to look forCheck which side is engraved: cutting on the engraving can damage it, so many are best kept for serving. Confirm spelling on the proof and read the care instructions for wood.

08

Beginner knitting kit

Everything needed for a first simple project, such as a scarf.

What to look forCheck that the yarn weight and needle size match the pattern, and whether a video or written guide is included. A small first project is more likely to be finished.
