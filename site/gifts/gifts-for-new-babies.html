Ad · Gift ideas for a new baby

By Callum Starr, NOAH Neurotech Ltd · Updated 6 November 2026

A spare for the changing bag, a book to read together or somewhere to keep the first photographs: a new-baby gift need not be elaborate. Ask what the parents would find useful. This guide covers everyday gifts and keepsakes, rather than sleep, feeding or medical equipment.

Ideas are selected from fixed gift-concept lists for the topic and budget, with practical buying checks. We have not bought or tested the products in these searches.

  • Ask before buying feeding, travel or sleep equipment. Those choices need to fit the family's routine and current safety guidance.
  • If buying clothes, ask which sizes they need and consider the season when the baby will wear them. They may already have plenty of newborn outfits.
  • Books and toys are for supervised time together, not for leaving in a baby's sleep space. Keepsake boxes and photo props belong with the adults.

01

Soft crinkle cloth book

A book to hold and explore together, with pictures to talk about as the baby grows.

What to look forCheck the stated age suitability and how it can be washed. Avoid loose accessories, long cords or detachable small parts, and choose a size that is easy for an adult to hold open.

02

Muslin squares

An extra supply for the changing bag or laundry rotation, if the parents use them.

What to look forFollow the parents' lead on fabric and size. Frequent washing is part of the job, so choose a pack whose care instructions fit their usual laundry routine.

03

Baby keepsake box

Somewhere for first cards, photographs and the small things the family wants to keep.

What to look forCheck the internal dimensions rather than the size of the packaging. Leave room for cards and photographs. This is storage for adults to keep out of the baby's reach, not a toy.

04

Soft rattle

A simple toy for a grown-up to use during short play sessions with the baby.

What to look forChoose a toy with clear age guidance and care instructions from an identifiable seller. Avoid loose parts and check stitching regularly; a soft covering alone does not establish suitability.

05

Hooded baby bath towel

A spare for bath time, without having to predict how quickly the baby will outgrow an outfit.

What to look forUse the towel's unfolded measurements, not the age range or packaging, to judge its size. Ask whether the family needs another, and choose one that fits their washing routine.

06

Baby milestone cards

For parents who enjoy taking photographs to remember the first year.

What to look forRead the milestones included in the pack and consider whether they suit the family. These are photo props for adults to handle, not objects to leave with the baby.

07

Board book for shared reading

A short story or book of pictures that the family can return to together.

What to look forLook for sturdy pages, a manageable size and clear age guidance. A few simple pictures may be easier to share than a busy book with flaps and loose extras.

08

Tea or coffee for the parents

Something for the adults to enjoy as well as a present for the new arrival.

What to look forTheir usual tea or coffee is a useful clue; ask about decaf and any dietary needs. If choosing coffee, buy beans only if they have a grinder, otherwise match the grind to their brewer.
