Gift ideas for 4-year-olds

At four, a favourite game might be building a tower, running a pretend cafe or asking for the same story again. Choose something that joins in with their play. Here are eight ideas, with advice on finding the right level of challenge.

Before you choose

Narrow down
the options.

  • Check the age label on the box. If the child has younger siblings, keep small-part toys well away from them.
  • Ask what they already enjoy and which building blocks or characters they have. An addition to a much-used set can be more welcome than a new system.
  • Think about the parents too: noise, storage and how much help a toy needs all affect whether it gets used.

01

Chunky wooden jigsaw puzzle

A good choice if they enjoy matching shapes and seeing a picture come together.

What to look forAsk what size puzzle they can finish now, then choose a similar challenge. A clear picture and a tray help them get started without an adult sorting every piece.

02

Dressing-up costume

A firefighter, a dragon or a character of their own invention: choose a costume they can make a story around.

What to look forA simple tabard they can put on themselves leaves more time for playing. Buy by the child's measurements, and look for easy fastenings and a washable fabric.

03

Large building blocks

For making the garage, castle or very tall tower they have in mind.

What to look forMatch the block system they already use: similar-looking pieces are not always compatible. Check the age label and the number of building pieces, not just the accessories.

04

Picture book

Pick a story about something they currently love, whether that is dinosaurs, trains or a mischievous dog.

What to look forRead a few pages aloud if you can: rhythm, repetition and things to spot give you plenty to share. Ask which titles the family already has before ordering.

05

Play-dough set

They can make a bakery today and a dinosaur tomorrow, using the same tubs.

What to look forCheck the age label, ingredients and allergy guidance. Resealable tubs and a few sturdy tools are more useful than lots of small accessories to lose.

06

Children's watercolour paint set

For a child who likes choosing colours and making their own pictures.

What to look forLook for age-appropriate paints with clear washing guidance, and include thick paper. Check that the set has a usable brush and a lid or tray for mixing colours.

07

Balance bike

Worth considering if they enjoy being outdoors and are ready for their first bike.

What to look forAgree this one with the parents before buying. Fit depends on the child's height and the bike's minimum seat height, not just the age on the listing. Allow for storage and protective equipment.

08

First board game

A game to play together, with short rounds so they can ask for another go.

What to look forChoose rules you can explain in a few sentences. Check the player count, playing time and whether every turn needs reading, counting or help from an adult.
