Gift ideas for dad

Think about how your dad spends a free afternoon. A project he is working on, a subject he keeps reading about or an everyday item that is wearing out can point you towards a gift. Here are eight options, from something to take on a walk to a game you can play together.

  • Ask which part of a hobby he enjoys before buying equipment. Owning a set of tools does not mean he wants another one.
  • Check compatibility with what he owns: charging connections, storage sizes and existing speaker systems can rule out an otherwise useful gift.
  • For food, drink or an experience, check preferences, dietary needs and dates. None is automatically a safer choice than a physical gift.

01

Small-parts organiser

For the screws, fittings or spare pieces that need a better home than a mixed drawer.

What to look forMeasure what he needs to store and where the box will live. Removable dividers help with different-sized parts; check that small items cannot spill between compartments when it is carried.

02

Insulated flask

For a drink on a walk, at an outdoor match or during a day away from the kettle.

What to look forChoose capacity around the number of drinks he wants to take. Check empty weight, lid design and cleaning instructions; a large flask with a cup lid serves a different purpose from a travel mug.

03

History book

More on the period or event he stops to read about whenever it comes up.

What to look forAsk what he wants to know more about, then look through sample pages. A readable account of one event may suit him better than a broad overview; a glance at his shelves will help avoid duplicates.

04

Rechargeable head torch

Light for a job or outdoor activity where he needs both hands free.

What to look forSimple controls and a useful low setting can matter more than maximum brightness. Choose a weight he will be happy wearing, and confirm the charging connection fits the cables he uses.

05

Whisky tasting glasses

A pair of glasses for comparing whiskies he already enjoys.

What to look forTake a cue from the glasses he uses: a tasting shape and a heavy tumbler feel quite different. Confirm the quantity and washing instructions. The search is for glasses, not whisky.

06

Wallet

A replacement if his current one is worn and you know which layout he likes.

What to look forCheck whether he uses notes, coins and cards, then compare dimensions with his current wallet. Keep the receipt and match his material preference rather than assuming leather or a slimmer design is better.

07

Two-player board game

A game to open and play together, with no need to gather a large group.

What to look forLook for accounts of playing with exactly two people, not just a box marked 'two or more'. Choose a theme and rules both of you will enjoy, with time for a full game.

08

Portable Bluetooth speaker

For music or spoken-word audio somewhere his usual speakers do not reach.

What to look forAsk what he uses now and whether it needs to connect to an existing system. Compare weight, charging connection and stated battery life; check any water-resistance rating for the intended location.
