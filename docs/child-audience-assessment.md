# Intended audience and Children's Code assessment

Assessment: 6 November 2026. Operator and controller: NOAH Neurotech Ltd.
This is an initial product assessment, not legal advice or proof that the
Children's Code is inapplicable. The operator should obtain a privacy review.

## Intended use and evidence

Santa's Helper is a shopping research tool for adults aged 18 and over buying
for others, including children. The finder, Privacy and Terms state this before
submission. Recipient age fields describe the person receiving a gift, not
permission for a child to use the service. Fixed guides discuss parents,
compatibility, budgets, delivery and buying checks. There are no child accounts,
child-directed games, rewards, chat personas, social messaging or behavioural
advertising profiles.

Those features support an adult audience, but Christmas branding, toy guides,
free access and the lack of age verification mean children could still visit.
An 18+ sentence alone does not establish that use by under-18s is unlikely.
There is not yet a real audience study or reliable age-distribution evidence.
Do not state that the service is proven not likely to be accessed by children.

## Measures already in place

- Ask shoppers not to submit names, identifying or sensitive details.
- Apply deterministic age and restricted-product checks to ideas and listings.
- No accounts, recipient profiles, targeted ads or uploaded wishlists.
- Limit feedback to signed controlled labels, with expiry and replay protection.
- Keep saved lists on the device; explain sharing and local-storage limitations.
- Explain AI involvement, third parties, retention, lawful bases and rights.

These are safeguards, not an age-assurance system. They do not remove the need
to assess the ICO's actual "likely to be accessed" test.

## Required follow-up and review triggers

Before any child-directed promotion, teenage-user feature or app-store release,
the operator must review the ICO guidance and Anthropic's requirements for
products used by minors. Ask a qualified adviser whether a DPIA, stronger age
assurance or Children's Code measures are required. Obtain privacy-preserving
audience evidence from real usability research rather than inferring ages from
gift-recipient fields or collecting children's dates of birth.

Review after initial adult-user research, any credible reports of child use,
new social/personalisation features, targeted advertising, or changed AI terms.
If meaningful teenage use is expected, do not rely on the current adult-only
position: implement and publish the fuller safeguards before enabling it.
